A rigged market

In 1950s Dublin and Derry, working-class poverty was engineered by predatory credit.

Commercial banks demanded property deeds that tenement families did not own. Licensed lenders operated up to the 39 percent threshold of the Moneylenders Act 1933, while street collectors charged a shilling in the pound each week: 260 percent simple annual interest.

Without a government grant or a single riot, Nora Herlihy, Seán Forde, and Séamus P. MacEoin reverse-engineered cooperative finance from Nova Scotia and built a rival ledger on Donore Avenue on 26 April 1958.
